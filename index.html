🧪 Let's learn mixing together!

  1. Meet the droplets
  2. Stir
  3. Add diffusion
  4. 🎓
👆 drag to stir

1 · Meet the droplets

Here are two droplets, one red and one blue, sitting side by side. Right now they are completely segregated: each colour has its own space.

How do they become one uniform mixture? Let's find out.

2 · Stir (no diffusion yet)

Drag inside the box to stir, or let me stir for you.

Watch closely: stirring does not blend the colours. It stretches and folds the interface between them into longer and thinner sheets, while the colours stay perfectly pure.

Interface stretched ×1.0 Still unmixed 100%

🤯 Wow, stretched ×100! The sheets are now thinner than a hair. Stirring alone can't do more: time to add diffusion!

3 · Switch on diffusion

Now molecular diffusion joins in. It is slow over long distances, but the sheets you made are so thin that it smooths them out quickly, and the colours finally blend.

Keep stirring: the thinner the sheets, the faster they mix!

Still unmixed 100%

🎉 You just did mixing science!

What you saw is the fundamental picture behind all of mixing: stirring stretches the interface between the substances (Ottino, 1989), and diffusion then erases the thin sheets it creates.

As Villermaux & Duplat (2003) put it, a mixture is only a transient state between the initial segregation of the ingredients and their final homogeneity.

This idea, stretching plus diffusion, is the foundation of mixing science, and it is exactly what I study inside porous rocks. 🪨

References
Ottino, J. M. (1989). The Kinematics of Mixing: Stretching, Chaos, and Transport. Cambridge University Press.
Villermaux, E. & Duplat, J. (2003). Mixing as an aggregation process. Physical Review Letters, 91, 184501.
